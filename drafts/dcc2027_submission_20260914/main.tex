\begin{abstract}
Native spatial mergers already compress visual patches before a
vision-language model (VLM) reaches its language model, yet most token
selectors rank the merger outputs. We call compression \emph{machine-centric}
when its units, budget, and interface follow the deployed model's native
computation graph. Rank-Before-Merge (RBM) is a training-free rule that ranks
complete native units before applying the unmodified merger. At 25\% retention,
RBM outperforms matched post-merger
L2 selection on all nine text-dense full-split cells across Qwen3-VL,
Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL3, with gains of 11.0--38.4 percentage points and
235--432 OCRBench points. A merger-aware rank prior also improves FastV-k3 on
all four locked Qwen3-VL probes at the same budget, although only TextVQA is
significant and pure RBM remains stronger on OCRBench. Rank and token-survival
analyses attribute the main effect to merger-induced saliency rewriting. RBM
adds no learned parameters and increases measured throughput by 68\% on
Qwen3-VL at 25\% retention.
\end{abstract}

\Section{Introduction}

Modern VLMs can expose hundreds to tens of thousands of visual tokens to the
language model. Reducing that sequence lowers attention and key--value-cache
cost, so visual-token compression has become an important part of efficient
multimodal inference. On our A40 system, reducing the visual-token rate to
25\% lowers Qwen3-VL latency from 0.36 to 0.23 seconds and raises throughput by
68\%. The central question is which visual evidence should occupy that limited
rate.

Merger-equipped models introduce a structural detail
that current methods often treat as fixed plumbing: neighboring patch features
are first compressed into native spatial units, and a later selector decides
which merged tokens survive. If the merger changes saliency order, a precise
top-$k$ decision made after it cannot recover an answer-bearing unit that was
ranked below the threshold.

We call a compressor \emph{machine-centric} when its selection unit, budget,
position handling, stream identity, and output interface follow the native
computation graph of the deployed model. This term does not imply a new
learned importance model. It distinguishes compression that respects the
machine's existing representation and topology from a selector applied to a
convenient but already altered token stream. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows
the resulting design: Rank-Before-Merge (RBM) evaluates complete $2\!\times\!2$
units on merger-input features, keeps a fixed per-image budget, and invokes the
original merger only on the survivors.

\end{figure}

Our first contribution is a representation-dependent formulation of visual
token selection around a lossy native merger. It states exactly when pre- and
post-merger top-$k$ decisions agree. Second, we introduce RBM as a training-free
compressor with an explicit native-unit and interface contract.
Across three VLMs and two merger designs, RBM preserves substantially more
text-dense evidence than matched post-merger selection at equal token count.
Third, we test whether the pre-merger signal complements a strong
query-conditioned selector. A locked RankBridge probe reserves 20\% of the
final budget for pre-merger-ranked units and fills the remaining budget using
FastV attention. It improves FastV-k3 on all four tested Qwen3-VL benchmarks by
0.5--3.2 percentage points, with a significant TextVQA gain. This establishes
that pre-merger and query-conditioned ranks contain complementary signals.
Finally, controlled rank, edge-energy, and ranking-swap analyses connect the
accuracy gap to merger-induced saliency rewriting.

\Section{Related Work}

Existing methods intervene at different points in the inference graph. FastV
uses question-conditioned attention inside an early LLM layer
~\cite{chen2024fastv}; PyramidDrop progressively shortens the sequence across
LLM layers~\cite{xing2024pyramiddrop}; FasterVLM and SparseVLM refine
attention- and query-based selection
~\cite{zhang2024fastervlm,zhang2024sparsevlm}. VisionZip selects dominant
visual tokens and merges the remainder, while AdaptMerge studies adaptive
visual--language-guided merging
~\cite{yang2024visionzip,islam2025adaptmerge}. PruMerge, FitPrune, and recent
systems add learned, fitted, diversity, or text-guided importance signals
~\cite{shang2024prumerge,ye2024fitprune,yu2026visiontrim,
baek2026agilepruner,fang2026prunesid}.

These methods chiefly optimize \emph{which} token survives. We isolate
\emph{where in the representation path} the decision is made. The distinction
is material for Qwen-VL: its PatchMerger concatenates four neighboring patch
vectors, then applies LayerNorm and a nonlinear MLP. InternVL3 instead uses
pixel-shuffle downsampling
~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl,bai2025qwen25vl,zhu2025internvl3}. RBM keeps these native
operators and the downstream visual interface unchanged; it moves the ranking
decision to their input.

\Section{Machine-Centric Compression}

\SubSection{Selection around a native merger}

Let $U=\{u_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be the native merge units and let $M$ be the
model's per-unit merger. A representation-dependent scorer produces
$a_i=s_{\mathrm{pre}}(u_i)$ before merging and
$b_i=s_{\mathrm{post}}(M(u_i))$ after merging. At budget $k$, the two decisions
are
\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{pre}}=\operatorname{TopK}_{k}\{a_i\},\qquad
K_{\mathrm{post}}=\operatorname{TopK}_{k}\{b_i\}.
\label{eq:sets}
\end{equation}
With deterministic tie breaking, they are equivalent exactly when
$K_{\mathrm{pre}}=K_{\mathrm{post}}$. A sufficient condition for agreement at
every budget is $b_i=g(a_i)$ for a strictly increasing $g$. We measure the
violation at budget $k$ by
\begin{equation}
D_k=1-\frac{|K_{\mathrm{pre}}\cap K_{\mathrm{post}}|}
{|K_{\mathrm{pre}}\cup K_{\mathrm{post}}|}.
\label{eq:disagreement}
\end{equation}
For a fixed unit set, selection and a per-unit merger are compatible. The
non-equivalence arises because the merger changes the ranking that determines
the set.

For compression analysis, define the visual-token rate and task distortion as
\begin{equation}
R=\frac{k}{N},\qquad
\mathcal{D}(R)=1-\frac{S(R)}{S(1)},
\label{eq:rate_distortion}
\end{equation}
where $S(R)$ is the downstream benchmark score at rate $R$. A lower
$\mathcal{D}$ means that more task-relevant information survives. RBM and
Post-L2 have identical $R$, so their vertical separation isolates distortion
introduced by the representation used for selection.

\SubSection{Rank-Before-Merge}

For every image, RBM groups ViT patch features into the model's native
$2\!\times\!2$ units. For a unit $u$ with input patch vectors $h_{u,1:4}$, it
uses the query-blind control score
\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{pre}}(u)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\|h_{u,j}\|_2,
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{post}}(u)=\|M(h_{u,1:4})\|_2.
\end{equation}
It retains $k=\max(1,\operatorname{round}(\kappa N))$ units and applies the
native merger to those units. The method contract has four parts: selection
uses complete native units; every image receives its exact top-$k$ budget; the
same unit identities propagate through main and deepstack streams; and the
native merger, positional encoding, visual interface, and downstream LLM are
unchanged. L2 scoring costs $O(Nd)$ for $d$-dimensional features and top-$k$
selection costs $O(N\log k)$ with a heap. RBM adds no parameters and does not
require question tokens or an LLM-attention pass.

Because Qwen's merger is applied independently per unit, supplying a fixed
kept set to the original operator produces the same merged representations.
This is byte-identical in the verified Qwen3-VL path. The invariant lets us
separate a changed ranking from a changed forward operator in the swap control
below.

\SubSection{A merger-aware prior for FastV}

FastV ranks the full visual-token candidate set with question-conditioned
attention at LLM layer $K=3$. RankBridge tests whether the merger-input order
contains information that this later signal misses. At retention $\kappa=0.25$,
it protects $q=\operatorname{round}(\rho k)$ positions selected by the RBM
pre-rank and fills the remaining $k-q$ positions by FastV rank. A development
gate over $\rho\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$ selected $\rho=0.2$; the value was then
locked for four $n=200$ benchmarks. At $\rho=0$, the implementation is
bit-identical to FastV; at $\rho=1$, its kept set is identical to RBM. All
visual tokens remain available until layer $K$, so the pre-rank does not starve
FastV's candidate set.

\Section{Experimental Protocol}

We evaluate Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and InternVL3-8B. The Qwen models use a
learned PatchMerger and InternVL3 uses pixel-shuffle downsampling. All runs use
bf16 weights, greedy decoding, and family-correct native positions. RBM, no-
compression, and Post-L2 cells use vLLM 0.19 V1~\cite{kwon2023vllm}; FastV and
RankBridge use a HuggingFace eager-attention harness because FastV has no vLLM
path. The eager implementation reproduces the native path on 8/8 per-sample
checks, and HF/vLLM no-compression outputs agree on 16/16 held-out cases.
Throughput comparisons remain within vLLM.

The benchmarks are TextVQA with official VQA accuracy
~\cite{singh2019textvqa}, DocVQA with ANLS~\cite{mathew2021docvqa}, OCRBench
with its /1000 score~\cite{liu2024ocrbench}, and GQA with normalized exact
match~\cite{hudson2019gqa}. Full splits contain 5000, 5349, 1000, and 12578
items. Scorers are ports of the lmms-eval implementations
~\cite{zhang2024lmmseval}. Pre/post comparisons pair identical samples and
per-image token counts. The nine prespecified text-dense comparisons at 25\%
retention use 20,000-resample paired-bootstrap intervals and paired sign-flip
tests with Holm correction; every corrected $p$ is at most
$4.5\!\times\!10^{-4}$. FastV and RankBridge comparisons are secondary locked
probes and use paired tests on $n=200$.

\Section{Compression Results}

\SubSection{Matched pre- and post-merger selection}

Table~\ref{tab:main} isolates selection stage within the same query-blind L2
family. At 25\% retention, RBM outperforms Post-L2 in all nine text-dense cells.
The Qwen3-VL gains are 38.4\pp on TextVQA, 24.3\pp on DocVQA, and 363
OCRBench points; Qwen2.5-VL gains 26.1\pp, 11.0\pp, and 298 points. The
direction replicates with InternVL3's different merger: 37.4\pp, 34.6\pp, and
432 points. Deeper 12.5\% retention widens the gap on the reported text-dense
cells. RBM is not lossless relative to no compression, especially on
InternVL3 DocVQA and OCRBench. The evidence establishes improved retention over
a matched post-merger decision.

GQA gives the boundary of the result. The operational Qwen rankings favor

\SubSection{FastV and the merger-aware prior}

\Section{Why the Merger Changes Selection}

Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} summarizes three diagnostics on Qwen3-VL. First,
pre- and post-merger unit ranks are weakly related. Spearman correlation is
0.137 on DocVQA, 0.332 on TextVQA, and 0.360 on GQA; top-25\% kept-set Jaccard
is 0.180, 0.243, and 0.278. The native merger therefore changes the actual
top-$k$ decision, not only score scale.

Second, the changed order is structured. With rank shift defined as post rank
minus pre rank, its correlation with Sobel edge energy is $+0.439$ on DocVQA,
$+0.155$ on TextVQA, and $+0.036$ on GQA. On DocVQA, units kept by RBM but
dropped by Post-L2 have mean edge energy 0.641, compared with 0.124 in the
reverse group; 92\% lie above the per-image median, compared with 35\%. This
supports text-stroke demotion as a mechanism-level association. It does not
establish the merger's training objective as the cause.

Third, a ranking-swap control holds the post-merger forward path fixed but uses
the pre ranking. On Qwen3-VL, it selects exactly the RBM set
(Jaccard $=1.000$) and recovers pre accuracy, while the retained merged outputs
are byte-identical. The measured Qwen3-VL gap is therefore attributable to the
selection ranking rather than a different operation on the kept units.

\Section{Efficiency and Scope}

Efficiency is measured in vLLM 0.19 V1 on one A40 GPU. Across 75\%, 50\%, and
25\% retention, RBM and Post-L2 throughput is 4.61/4.52, 5.39/5.54, and
6.91/6.89 requests/s. Five-repeat measurements remain within 3\% with no
consistent stage difference. Relative to no compression, RBM raises
throughput by 12\%, 31\%, and 68\%; at 25\% retention, latency falls from 0.36
to 0.23 seconds. InternVL3 independently yields 1.8--2.5$\times$ throughput at
25\% retention. RBM does not reduce ViT computation before its scoring tap;
its gain comes from shortening the sequence presented to the native merger and
language model.

The byte-exact ranking attribution is established on Qwen3-VL. Qwen2.5-VL
preserves kept-set identity but has an ordering confound, and InternVL3 offers
accuracy-level replication and a bounded swap result. Models without an
identifiable native merge unit are outside the method's scope. FastV and
RankBridge accuracy uses a different eager harness from the vLLM efficiency
study. Several secondary FastV and mechanism cells use $n=200$, as marked, and
only the TextVQA RankBridge gain is significant. Pixel caps differ across some
model families, so absolute cross-model scores should not be compared. The
current result supports a merger-aware machine-centric design rule for
text-dense compression, not a universal ranking stage or selector.

\Section{Conclusion}

Visual-token selection around a native merger is an operator-order problem.
RBM resolves it by selecting complete native units on merger-input features
and applying the unchanged model only to the survivors. Under equal token
budgets, this training-free design preserves substantially more text-dense
evidence than matched Post-L2 across three VLMs and two merger designs while
delivering the expected inference speedup. A locked merger-aware rank prior
also improves FastV-k3 on all four tested Qwen3-VL probes, indicating that
pre-merger and query-conditioned ranks contribute complementary information.
The mechanism results show why: the native merger changes saliency order and
preferentially demotes text-stroke units. Compression methods for
merger-equipped VLMs should therefore treat native topology and selection stage
as first-class design constraints.
