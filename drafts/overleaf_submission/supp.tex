\subsection*{S4b. Cascade two-stage gate --- paired McNemar counts (Qwen3-VL-8B, $n = 200$, official rescore)}

keeping fraction $X$ of merger-input units, Stage~2 $=$ FastV at LLM layer~2

\noindent\textbf{S4c.2 Adaptive stage router} (body \S\,6f): query-blind
per-image workload detector on merger-input features --- high-frequency tail
ratio $r_{\mathrm{HF}} = \Pr\big[\mathrm{Var}_u > \bar{\mathrm{Var}} +
\sigma_{\mathrm{Var}}\big]$ and 20-bin L2-entropy $H(\|f_u\|_2)$ --- routing
each image to PRE (RBM, rank-before-merge) or POST (FastV, merge-then-rank),
both iso-budget. $\kappa$ is the kept fraction; the winner of a
$\tau_{\mathrm{HF}}{\times}\tau_{\mathrm{ent}}$ grid on TextVQA$_{500}$
($\tau_{\mathrm{HF}}{=}0.08$, $\tau_{\mathrm{ent}}{=}2.0$) routes every image to
PRE, reproducing plain RBM bit-for-bit: text benchmarks show $0.00$~pp
regression but GQA gain is also $+0.00$~pp (below the required $+1$~pp). The
$\tau_{\mathrm{HF}}{=}0.13$ threshold does route some scene images to POST but
collapses TextVQA (51/320 flipped, $-7$~pp) while flipping only 22/243 GQA
images ($0.428$ vs RBM $0.438$). GQA and TextVQA $r_{\mathrm{HF}}$ distributions
overlap (means 0.127 vs 0.142), so no single global threshold reconciles the two
acceptance criteria. Evidence record: audit index entry for the
adaptive stage-router gate and workload-signal analysis.

\noindent\textbf{S4c.3 Exploratory learned boundary scorer} (body \S\,6):
we test whether a lightweight learned selector can improve on plain L2 without
moving selection past the merger. A small MLP receives only pre-merger
boundary features available at inference time: unit L2 norm, Sobel edge energy,
local feature variance, normalized position, and neighborhood statistics. Its
teacher is the post-merger importance ordering captured on separate training
images; evaluation uses disjoint held-out benchmark samples and the official
task scorers. Both learned and L2 arms retain exactly 25\% of merger-input
units and otherwise share the same Qwen3-VL-8B inference configuration.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}l r r r@{}}
    \toprule
    Benchmark & Learned POST teacher & Plain L2 & $\Delta$ learned$-$L2 \\
    \midrule
    TextVQA & 0.435 & \textbf{0.595} & $-16.0$~pp \\
    GQA     & \textbf{0.550} & 0.515 & $+3.5$~pp \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Held-out learned-scorer evaluation at 25\% retention
  ($n=200$ per benchmark; official metrics). The scorer can imitate a
  post-merger teacher, but the resulting ordering is strongly anti-task on
  TextVQA and therefore fails the cross-workload no-regression gate.}
  \label{tab:supp_learned}
\end{table}

The asymmetric task result is diagnostic rather than a method improvement.
M2 independently shows that the post-merger ordering demotes
text-stroke-rich units; distillation transfers this preference into a
pre-merger scorer and causes a 16.0-pp TextVQA loss. The positive GQA result
shows that the learned head is capable of changing task behavior, ruling out a
trivial ``model did not learn'' explanation. We therefore retain plain L2 and
do not use the learned scorer in any headline table.

\subsection*{S7b. Pure-stage confound control --- pre-final (Qwen3-VL-8B, $n = 200$)}

The body \S\,4.2 pre-final control isolates the \emph{stage} from the
feature-space confound of the original pre (deepstack[0]-input, layer~8) vs
post (main-merger output) contrast. pre-final ranks L2 at the main-merger's
\emph{own input} (final ViT-block features) and selects the top-$\kappa$
merge-units from the visual output; the deepstack mergers run untouched. The
only difference from post is then before vs after the lossy $2\!\times\!2$ main
merger --- a pure stage variable. Iso-token verified (prompt token ids match

\begin{itemize}

\section*{S10. Qualitative same-image comparison --- measured pre- vs post-merger L2 selection}
\label{app:s10}

For four representative inputs we render, on the \emph{same} processor-resized
image, unit grid, $25\%$ budget and final visual-token count, the original
image with its full merge-unit grid, the RBM (pre-merger L2 top-$k$) kept mask,
the post-merger L2 top-$k$ kept mask (same L2 selector, same budget), and a
four-state difference map (kept-by-both / RBM-only / post-only / dropped-by-both,
encoded by colour \emph{and} hatch/outline so the states remain distinguishable
in greyscale). Each panel reports the measured top-$k$ Jaccard index
$J = |\text{pre} \cap \text{post}| / |\text{pre} \cup \text{post}|$ and the
full-ranking Spearman $\rho$ between the pre- and post-merger L2 orderings
(computed from the stored NPZ rank arrays; values cross-checked against the
validation report, $|\Delta| < 10^{-5}$). All scores are the target model's own
merger-input / merger-output feature L2 norms (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct,
\texttt{enforce\_eager}, \texttt{max\_pixels}$\,=\,$1.5M); no CLIP, SigLIP,
attention map, or fabricated saliency is used. The FastV layer-2 panel is
intentionally absent (no user-supplied question exists for these images; a
query-conditioned mask cannot be measured without one) and is recorded as
\texttt{fastv\_status: skipped\_missing\_question}.

The consistently low Jaccard ($0.25$--$0.35$) and moderate Spearman
($0.30$--$0.48$) across all ten captured inputs (four shown; the remaining six
ship in the code release) are the image-level signature of the body paper's
mechanism result: the learned $2\!\times\!2$ merger \emph{reshuffles} the
unit ranking, so selecting before versus after it on the same image with the
same selector and budget keeps largely \emph{different} units. The four-state
maps show that the disagreement is spatially structured (contiguous RBM-only
and post-only regions), not uniform noise, and that the ``kept-by-both''
intersection is a sparse minority of the budget.
